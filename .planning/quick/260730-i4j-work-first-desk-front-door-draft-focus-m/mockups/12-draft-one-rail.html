The Accountant Who Wouldn’t Retire

Margaret Osei spent thirty-one years reconciling other people’s ledgers. At sixty-two she started keeping her own.

Margaret Osei was born in 1961 in Kumasi, the fourth of six children, and by her own account was the only one who enjoyed arithmetic. Her father kept the accounts for a timber concern; she learned double-entry bookkeeping at the kitchen table before she learned to ride a bicycle.

She qualified in 1986, joined a mid-tier firm in Accra, and stayed for three decades. Colleagues describe a person who was never once late and never once loud. There is no origin myth here, no moment of conversion — only a woman who noticed, slowly, that the audits she signed off were describing places she had never been.

The ledger nobody kept

In 2019 she took early retirement and spent four months doing what she called a truly remarkable piece of work — walking eleven districts and counting, by hand, the number of village schools with no roof.

The resulting document ran to four hundred pages. It was, in form and in temperament, an audit. She sent it to the district assembly, who thanked her, and to the ministry, who did not reply. So she began raising the money herself, and has not stopped since.
