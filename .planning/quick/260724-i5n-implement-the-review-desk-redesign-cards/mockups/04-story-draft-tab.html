Four Thousand Cemeteries, No One on Payroll

The state has four thousand burial grounds and a maintenance plan for none of them.

There are more dead Vermonters than living ones, and the living have stopped keeping track. The state maintains a registry of 4,100 known burial grounds, a number its own historians describe as an undercount. Responsibility for these sites is distributed among 251 town clerks, an unknown number of church boards, and several hundred families who no longer exist.

The Arithmetic of Neglect

A cemetery requires roughly $400 a year to keep from disappearing — mowing, fence repair, the occasional resetting of a stone that frost has pushed over. Multiplied across the full backlog, the cost of maintaining every abandoned cemetery in Vermont is less than the state spends on one highway interchange.

Must fix Accuracy · Fact Guard Research doc cites $460/yr (VOCA 2024 survey), not $400. Figure appears once more in the case study. Suggested: "roughly $460 a year"

The Society's own books, published annually since 1989, list every dollar. Last year it spent $61,000 across 214 sites and apologized, in print, for the overhead of a single part-time bookkeeper.

Perpetual Care, Briefly

The trusts that were supposed to fund mowing in perpetuity ran out of perpetuity around 1974. A perpetual-care fund established in 1911 with $200 now yields approximately $3.80 a year, which at current rates purchases eleven minutes of professional mowing.
